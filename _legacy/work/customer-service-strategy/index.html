CASE 02 / CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE · SERVICE STRATEGY

Making customer differences useful for service decisions.

For Braskem’s Netherlands headquarters, a CX workstream explored how customer needs, purchasing behaviour and operational realities could guide a more differentiated service offer.

Challenge

Make customer differences meaningful for service and account decisions.

Contribution

Connect interviews, customer journeys, blueprints and commercial data.

Delivered

Customer archetypes, operational blueprints and six evolving customer clusters.

CONTEXT

Braskem · Netherlands headquarters

MY FOCUS

CX strategy, research & data analysis

SCOPE

B2B customer relationships

TIMEFRAME

12-month initiative

At a glanceDecisions & project materialOutputs & outcomes

THE BUSINESS QUESTION

Which customer differences should influence the service we offer, and how should teams act on them?

My contribution

My role connected customer experience, data analysis and strategy. The work combined customer and Account Manager interviews, journeys, blueprints and cross-functional discussions to support a classification that teams could use in their decisions.

Collaboration

Product Manager, CX Strategist, Market Analyst and Scrum Master, working with commercial and operational stakeholders.

12months of research and development
6customer clusters in the model
1,000+customers processed monthly

01 / DECISION

Start with the decisions the service must support

Define the service and commercial decisions the customer classification must support.

Read the decision context ＋

The classification needed to inform everyday decisions across teams. The initiative considered customer relationships, service benefits and differentiators, commercial priority and raw-material allocation. Two workshops moved from a broad set of variables towards a more focused basis for customer grouping.

Segmentation had to support decisions, beyond describing customers.

02 / DECISION

Connect the customer journey to internal delivery

Map the full customer journey alongside the internal work needed to deliver it.

Read the decision context ＋

Customer journeys explored purchasing, contact channels, friction and post-purchase experiences. Blueprints added the work behind those interactions across Customer Service, Supply Chain, Logistics and Sales. Customer and Account Manager interviews informed behavioural archetypes.

The customer experience depended on several areas working together.

03 / DECISION

Build a classification that could evolve

Make the segmentation updateable as commercial behaviour changes.

Read the decision context ＋

The project’s K-Means model used commercial data to generate monthly segmentation updates. More than 25 variables were considered, with 10 prioritised. A simple upload flow used Excel exports from sources such as SAP. The model’s technical development was a team deliverable; my contribution centred on CX, research, analysis and strategy.

A useful account strategy needs a way to respond to changing customer behaviour.

OUTPUTS & OUTCOMES

What the work made possible.

The project delivered customer journeys, operational blueprints, archetypes and a segmentation model processing more than 1,000 customers monthly. These outputs supported account management and service prioritisation. Forecast improvements belonged to a proposed future phase and are not presented as achieved results.

What I take forward

Customer classification becomes a service design tool when it connects customer differences to the teams, processes and choices that shape the experience.
