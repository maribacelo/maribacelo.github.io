CASE 01 / SERVICE DESIGN · OPERATING MODELS

Connecting five innovation programmes into a clearer service.

An innovation hub supported students, startups and brand owners through five programmes. The challenge was to understand how those services connected, and give teams practical ways to manage them.

Challenge

Five programmes with different partners, processes and participant journeys.

Contribution

Connect participant research with programme operations and responsibilities.

Delivered

Five mapped journeys, process flowcharts and a tailored management toolbox.

CONTEXT

Braskem · Innovation ecosystem

MY FOCUS

Service design, research & process mapping

SCOPE

Five programmes

TIMEFRAME

Programme discovery & design

At a glanceDecisions & project materialOutputs & outcomes

THE BUSINESS QUESTION

How can five programmes work as a connected service, for participants and the teams delivering them?

My contribution

My work focused on connecting participant research with the operational view: making journeys, dependencies and management needs visible, then translating them into tools and process guidance with the project team.

Collaboration

A collaborative initiative involving programme teams and participants across the innovation ecosystem.

5programme journeys mapped
14stakeholder interviews
35market players benchmarked

01 / DECISION

See the whole ecosystem

Map the connections between programmes before designing individual solutions.

Read the decision context ＋

The programmes had different partners, activities and relationships. Mapping the business model and operational ecosystem created a view of how they connected, rather than treating each programme as an isolated offer. Benchmarking helped the team compare approaches across 35 market players.

The service needed a shared view before it needed more tools.

02 / DECISION

Bring the participant experience into operations

Bring the need for follow-up into the service journey, beyond participation.

Read the decision context ＋

Interviews with students, startups and brand owners explored experiences before, during and after participation. A need for follow-up and continuity emerged. Programme journeys connected those participant needs to internal processes and opportunities for improvement.

Participation does not end when an event or programme ends.

03 / DECISION

Make the work usable by delivery teams

Pair journey maps with ownership, routines and practical delivery tools.

Read the decision context ＋

The team developed programme flowcharts and a toolbox covering responsibilities, skills, documentation and planning. Tools such as RACI and skill mapping helped make ownership explicit. Implementation dossiers connected the proposed tools to the context of each programme.

A useful service model needs owners, routines and something teams can work with.

CONNECTING THE PERSPECTIVES

Experience.
Operations.
Strategy.

01Experience＋

Participant needs included continuity and follow-up, extending the service beyond the programme itself.

02Operations＋

Programme flows, responsibilities and tool guidance made internal dependencies visible.

03Strategy＋

A connected view of the five programmes created a foundation for coordinating and evolving the offer.

OUTPUTS & OUTCOMES

What the work made possible.

Five programme journeys, process flowcharts and a tailored management toolbox gave the team a structured foundation for coordinating the services. These are documented project outputs; long-term operational impact has not been quantified here.

What I take forward

The central lesson was to connect the experience offered to participants with the work needed to sustain it. Journey maps became more useful when paired with responsibilities and delivery tools.
